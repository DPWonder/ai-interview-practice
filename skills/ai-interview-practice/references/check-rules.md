# The quick check, in plain words

This is the quick check from Aethel's free practice (https://aethel.pro/practice), written out so
an agent can run it. It is simple word rules. It looks for signs in the words, not for meaning,
so it can be wrong both ways: a vague answer can still get ticks, and a good answer said in other
words can miss one. It is not a score.

The four signs mirror the things Aethel's published scoring rules look for in an answer: a
specific case, why you chose what you did, and how it turned out, plus your own part.

## 1. Does it count as an answer?

- It needs at least **8 words**.
- It must be more than readiness, small talk or a request to repeat. "OK", "Yes, I'm ready",
  "Sounds good", "Can you repeat that?" do not count, even with extra filler words.
- If it does not count, say: "Under 8 words, or only a short reply, so this would not count as an
  answer." Then the tip: "Write a few full sentences about one real case: what happened, what you
  did, why, and how it turned out." Skip the four signs.

## 2. The four signs

Check each one by the words listed, not by what you think the answer means. Go through the
answer sentence by sentence for each sign before you say it is missing.

If a sign is not spotted but you can see the answer says it in other words, keep the "not
spotted" line and add one sentence: 'Your words "..." may already say it; the word rule does not
catch them.' Do not mark it spotted, and do not contradict the line. Then pick the "Try this"
for the next missing sign, if there is one. When a sign is found, quote the sentence that holds it, word for word (cut a
long sentence to about 16 words around the sign, with "..." where you cut). Try to quote a
different sentence for each sign, so the check does not show one sentence four times.

### A real example (one specific case)

Spotted when the answer has either:

- a time phrase that dates one case: "last March", "last year", "this sprint", "in 2024",
  "during my internship", "my first job", "one night", "yesterday", "once", "at the time",
  a weekday or a month name; or
- a place, project or system word ("the checkout service", "our API", "a startup", "the
  pipeline", "production", "a bootcamp") **together with a number anywhere in the answer**
  ("a 40-person startup", "2,000 requests a minute").

Not spotted when it reads like a habit: the answer has no digit and says how you usually work
("always", "usually", "generally", "typically", "whenever", "I believe", "best practice",
"industry standard").

- Missing line: "Not spotted: when it was, where, and how big. Add a date, the place or project,
  and a number, so it reads as one real case."
- Missing because it reads as a habit: "This reads like how you usually work, not one case. Name
  one time: when it was, where, and how big."

### What you did yourself

Spotted when "I" (or "I've", "I'd", "I had") is followed, within two words, by a past action:
"I traced", "I wrote", "I added", "I built", "I chose", "I ran", "I led", "I shipped", "I fixed",
"I had already tried". Not in a sentence about what you always do: "I always write tests" is a
habit, not one.

- Missing line: "Not found. Say what you did yourself that time, not only what the team did or
  what you usually do."

### Why you chose it

Spotted when a sentence has a reason word: "because", "since", "so that", "so we", "so I",
"in order to", "instead of", "rather than", "decided", "chose", "picked", "went with", "option",
"trade-off", "weighed", "compared", "considered", "the reason", "otherwise", "risk".

A reason word in a sentence that also names a standard or a belief does not count: "because
it is the industry standard", "best practice", "quality matters", "it's important", "that's how
it's done".

Use the "names a standard" line only when the answer has at least one reason word from the list
and every one of them sits in a sentence that names a standard. With no reason word at all, use
the plain missing line, even if the answer names a standard.

- Missing line: "Not found. Say why you chose what you did, and what else you considered."
- Missing because it names only a standard: "That names a standard, not your reason. Say why you
  chose it in that case, and what else you considered."

### How it turned out

Spotted when there is an outcome word: "dropped", "fixed", "solved", "reduced", "saved",
"improved", "shipped", "launched", "released", "deployed", "merged", "rolled back", "went from",
"after that", "as a result", "in the end", "since then", "ended up", "turned out", "faster",
"fewer", "no longer", "percent", "%"; or an everyday outcome ("the next morning", "they thanked
us", "still uses it", "calmed down", "worked out"); or, failing those, any digit (0 to 9; a
number written as a word does not count here). Read every sentence for these words, even a
sentence about something that did not happen ("if we shipped"): the rule matches words, so that
still counts. Not in a sentence about what you always do. Quote the latest sentence that has a number together with an
outcome word or "after", "since", "now", if there is one.

- Missing line: "We could not spot how it turned out. If you said it, great. If not, add one
  sentence on what happened in the end."

## 3. The longest sentence

Count the words in the longest sentence (sentences end at . ! ? or a line break).

- 20 words or more: "Your longest sentence has N words, long enough to back a top score."
- Fewer than 20: "Your longest sentence has N words. In Aethel's published rules, a score of 90
  or more needs a complete quote of at least 20 words."
- These are published rule numbers, not a score for the answer. Never turn them into one.

## 4. One "Try this" line

Pick the first sign that is missing, in the order above, and give its frame. The person fills in
the [brackets] from their own work. Never fill them in for them.

| Missing | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| Not an answer | Write a few full sentences about one real case: what happened, what you did, why, and how it turned out. |
| A real example (junior) | Open with when, where and how big: "In [month and year], for [the course, bootcamp, job or my own project], I built [what] in [how many weeks], for [who used it] ..." |
| A real example (other levels) | Open with when, where and how big: "In [month and year], on [where], with [how big], I ..." where [where] and [how big] fit the role: backend "[the service or API]", "[how many requests or users]"; frontend "[the page or component]", "[how many users]"; full-stack "[the feature]", "[how many users]"; data and ML "[the model or pipeline]", "[how much data]"; DevOps and SRE "[the system or pipeline]", "[how many services or deploys]"; mobile "[the app or screen]", "[how many users]". |
| What you did yourself | Say what you did yourself, in the first person: "I traced ..., I proposed ..., I wrote ..." |
| Why you chose it | Add one sentence on why: "I chose [one option] instead of [another] because [your reason]." |
| How it turned out | End with how it turned out: "After that, [what you measured] went from [before] to [after]." |
| Nothing missing, longest sentence under 20 words | Put your strongest point in one complete sentence of 20 words or more, so it can be quoted as evidence. |
| Nothing missing | The AI interviewer asks follow-up questions, so be ready to go one layer deeper: one option you rejected and why, or what you would do differently now. |

## In Spanish

The same rules, with Spanish signs. Use "usted".

- Counts as an answer: at least 8 words, more than "listo", "adelante", "¿puede repetir?".
- A real example: "el año pasado", "la semana pasada", "en mi primer trabajo", "en 2024",
  "durante", "una vez", "un día", a weekday or month name; or a place or system word ("en una
  empresa", "el servicio", "producción", "el pipeline") with a number in the answer. A habit
  with no digit: "siempre", "normalmente", "por lo general", "suelo", "creo que", "buenas
  prácticas".
- What you did yourself: a first-person past verb: "revisé", "decidí", "elegí", "hice",
  "construí", "arreglé", "migré", "probé", "medí", "agregué", or another verb ending in "-é" or
  "-í" in the past. Not in a sentence about what you always do.
- Why: "porque", "ya que", "para que", "en lugar de", "en vez de", "decidí", "elegí", "comparé",
  "consideré", "por eso", "así que", "la razón", "riesgo", "si no". A reason word in a sentence
  that names a standard ("es importante", "buenas prácticas", "estándar de la industria", "creo
  que") does not count. Use the "nombra una norma" line only when there is a reason word and
  every one sits in such a sentence; with no reason word, use the plain missing line.
- How it turned out: "bajó", "subió", "mejoró", "redujimos", "funcionó", "resolví", "lanzamos",
  "pasó de", "al final", "desde entonces", "después de eso", "ya no", "por ciento", "%",
  "al día siguiente", "se solucionó"; or failing those, any digit (0 to 9).

The lines, in Spanish:

- Heading: "Comprobación rápida (no es un puntaje)". Found: "Detectado:".
- Counts: "Cuenta como respuesta: N palabras." Not counted: "Tiene menos de 8 palabras, o es solo
  una respuesta corta, así que no contaría como respuesta."
- Labels: "Un ejemplo real", "Lo que hizo usted", "Por qué lo eligió", "Cómo terminó".
- Missing example: "No se detectó cuándo fue, dónde y de qué tamaño. Agregue una fecha, el lugar o
  el proyecto y un número, para que se lea como un caso real."
- Missing example, habit: "Esto suena a cómo trabaja normalmente, no a un caso. Nombre una vez
  concreta: cuándo fue, dónde y de qué tamaño."
- Missing own part: "No se encontró. Diga lo que hizo usted esa vez, no solo lo que hizo el equipo o
  lo que suele hacer."
- Missing why: "No se encontró. Diga por qué eligió lo que hizo y qué otra cosa consideró."
- Missing why, standard: "Eso nombra una norma, no su razón. Diga por qué lo eligió en ese caso y qué
  otra cosa consideró."
- Missing result: "No pudimos ver cómo terminó. Si lo dijo, muy bien. Si no, agregue una frase
  sobre lo que pasó al final."
- Longest sentence, short: "Su frase más larga tiene N palabras. En las reglas publicadas de
  Aethel, un puntaje de 90 o más necesita una cita completa de al menos 20 palabras."
- Longest sentence, long enough: "Su frase más larga tiene N palabras, lo bastante larga para
  respaldar un puntaje alto."
- Try this: "Pruebe esto". Each line below is said as written, frames in quotation marks:
  - Not an answer: Escriba unas frases completas sobre un caso real: qué pasó, qué hizo usted, por
    qué y cómo terminó.
  - Example, junior: Empiece con cuándo, dónde y de qué tamaño: "En [mes y año], para [el curso, el
    bootcamp, el trabajo o mi propio proyecto], construí [qué] en [cuántas semanas], para [quién
    lo usó] ..."
  - Example, other levels: Empiece con cuándo, dónde y de qué tamaño: "En [mes y año], en [dónde],
    con [qué tamaño], yo ..." Fill [dónde] and [qué tamaño] by role: backend [el servicio o la
    API], [cuántas solicitudes o usuarios]; frontend [la página o el componente], [cuántos
    usuarios]; full-stack [la función], [cuántos usuarios]; datos y ML [el modelo o el pipeline],
    [cuántos datos]; DevOps y SRE [el sistema o el pipeline], [cuántos servicios o despliegues];
    móvil [la app o la pantalla], [cuántos usuarios].
  - Own part: Diga lo que hizo usted, en primera persona: "Revisé ..., propuse ..., escribí ..."
  - Why: Agregue una frase sobre el porqué: "Elegí [una opción] en lugar de [otra] porque [su
    razón]."
  - Result: Termine con cómo resultó: "Después de eso, [lo que midió] pasó de [antes] a
    [después]."
  - Sentence length: Diga su punto más fuerte en una frase completa de 20 palabras o más, para que
    se pueda citar como evidencia.
  - Deeper: La IA entrevistadora hace preguntas de seguimiento, así que prepárese para ir un paso
    más allá: una opción que descartó y por qué, o qué haría distinto hoy.

## Worked example (made up)

**This answer is an example written for this file. It is not a real candidate.**

Question (backend, mid-level): "Walk me through an outage or a serious bug you helped resolve, from
the first alert to the fix. What did you check first, and why?"

Example answer:

> Last March our checkout API started timing out at lunch, with about 2,000 requests a minute. I
> traced the slow calls to one query that scanned the whole orders table. I added an index instead
> of a cache, because a cache would have shown stale prices and the index fixed the cause. After
> that, p95 latency went from 900 ms to 120 ms and the timeouts stopped.

The check:

  orders table"

  shown stale prices..."
- **How it turned out.** Spotted: "After that, p95 latency went from 900 ms to 120 ms and the
  timeouts stopped"
- Your longest sentence has 22 words, long enough to back a top score.
- **Try this:** The AI interviewer asks follow-up questions, so be ready to go one layer deeper: one
  option you rejected and why, or what you would do differently now.

index would not slow down writes to the orders table?"
