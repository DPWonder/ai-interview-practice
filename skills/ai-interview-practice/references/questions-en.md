# AI interview practice questions for developers

Four questions per role and level, the kind an AI screening interview asks: about one real case from your work, so an answer can show a specific case, what you did yourself, why you chose it, and how it turned out. Junior questions accept any real work: a job, an internship, a bootcamp, a course or a project of your own. These are the questions of Aethel's free practice. Text licensed CC BY 4.0.

## Backend

### Junior (up to 2 years, a bootcamp, or a career change)

1. Tell me about a backend feature or API you built, in a job, an internship, a bootcamp or your own project. What did it do, and which part did you write yourself?
2. Describe a bug in your backend code that took you a long time to find. How did you track down the cause, and how did you fix it?
3. Tell me about a time you had to decide how to store some data, for example which table, field or database. What did you choose, why, and how did it work out?
4. Tell me about a time someone reviewed your code and asked for changes. What did they ask for, what did you change, and what did you learn from it?

### Mid-level (2 to 4 years)

1. Tell me about a backend service you owned in production. What made it hard to run, and what did you personally change?
2. Walk me through an outage or a serious bug you helped resolve, from the first alert to the fix. What did you check first, and why?
3. Describe a design decision where you traded off speed, reliability, cost or simplicity. What options did you consider, and how did you know your choice was right?
4. Tell me about a time a deadline pushed against code quality. How did you keep the risk low, with tests, reviews or a rollout plan, and what happened?

### Senior (5 years or more)

1. Tell me about a backend system you designed or redesigned. What problem forced the change, which designs did you reject, and what were the results?
2. Describe a production incident you led. How did you coordinate the response, and what changed afterwards so it would not happen again?
3. Tell me about a time you scaled a system as usage grew. What broke or was about to break, what did you change, and how did you measure the effect?
4. Tell me about a technical decision you had to explain to people who are not engineers, such as a risk, a delay or a cost. How did you explain it, and what did they decide?

## Frontend

### Junior (up to 2 years, a bootcamp, or a career change)

1. Tell me about a page, component or small app you built, in a job, a bootcamp or your own project. What did you build yourself, and what was tricky about it?
2. Describe a UI bug that only showed up in some browsers, screen sizes or states. How did you find the cause, and how did you fix it?
3. Tell me about a time you turned a design or mockup into working code. What did you have to decide on your own, and why?
4. Tell me about feedback you got on your frontend code or on a feature you shipped. What did you change because of it?

### Mid-level (2 to 4 years)

1. Tell me about a frontend feature you owned from design to release. What made it hard, and what did you personally decide?
2. Describe a time you made a page or an app faster. How did you measure it before and after, and which change made the difference?
3. Tell me about a choice you made about state management, data fetching or component structure. What options did you weigh, and how did it hold up?
4. Tell me about a time you made a feature more accessible, or fixed an accessibility problem. What was wrong, what did you change, and how did you check it?

### Senior (5 years or more)

1. Tell me about a frontend architecture decision you led, such as a framework, a design system or a migration. Why did you make it, and what did it cost?
2. Describe a time a release broke something for users. How did you find out, how did you respond, and what did you change in how the team ships?
3. Tell me about a time you raised the quality bar across a team: testing, performance budgets, accessibility or code review. What did you change, and what was the result?
4. Tell me about a disagreement with design or product about what to build or how. How did you work it out, and what shipped in the end?

## Full-stack

### Junior (up to 2 years, a bootcamp, or a career change)

1. Tell me about an app you built end to end, in a job, a bootcamp or your own project. How did the frontend, the backend and the data fit together?
2. Describe a bug where the problem turned out to be in a different layer than you first thought. How did you trace it?
3. Tell me about a time you had to learn a new tool, library or language quickly to finish something. How did you learn it, and what did you ship?
4. Tell me about a time you got stuck and asked for help. What had you already tried, who did you ask, and what happened next?

### Mid-level (2 to 4 years)

1. Tell me about a feature you shipped across the frontend and the backend. What did you change in each layer, and why there?
2. Describe a time you changed an API or a database schema that other code depended on. How did you roll it out without breaking things?
3. Tell me about a trade-off between shipping fast and building it properly. What did you choose, why, and how did it turn out?
4. Tell me about working with product or design on a feature whose requirements were unclear. How did you decide what to build?

### Senior (5 years or more)

1. Tell me about a product or system you were responsible for end to end. What were the biggest technical risks, and how did you handle them?
2. Describe a time you split, merged or restructured services or parts of a codebase. What drove the change, and what was the result?
3. Tell me about a time you had to choose between building something yourselves and using an existing service or library. How did you decide, and would you decide the same now?
4. Tell me about a time you helped another developer grow, through reviews, pairing or mentoring. What did you do, and what changed for them?

## Data and ML

### Junior (up to 2 years, a bootcamp, or a career change)

1. Tell me about a data analysis or a model you built, in a job, a course or your own project. What question did it answer, and what did you do yourself?
2. Describe a time the data was messy or wrong. How did you notice, and what did you do about it?
3. Tell me about a time you chose one method or model over another. What did you compare, and why did you pick it?
4. Tell me about a time you explained a result or a chart to someone without a data background. How did you explain it, and what did they do with it?

### Mid-level (2 to 4 years)

1. Tell me about a model or a data pipeline you owned in production. What made it hard to keep working, and what did you change?
2. Describe a time a model or a metric looked good offline but did not hold up in production or with real users. What happened, and what did you do?
3. Tell me about a trade-off between accuracy, speed, cost or simplicity in a data or ML project. How did you decide?
4. Tell me about a time you worked with engineers or product people to get a model or an analysis into real use. What did you have to change?

### Senior (5 years or more)

1. Tell me about a data or ML system you designed. What were the main design choices, and which options did you reject?
2. Describe a time you found a serious problem in a model or a dataset, such as leakage, bias or drift. How did you find it, and what changed?
3. Tell me about a time you decided not to use machine learning, or to use a simpler method. Why, and how did it turn out?
4. Tell me about setting the evaluation plan for a model or an experiment. What did you measure, why those measures, and what did the results lead to?

## DevOps and SRE

### Junior (up to 2 years, a bootcamp, or a career change)

1. Tell me about a deployment, a CI pipeline or a server setup you worked on, in a job, a course or your own project. What did you set up yourself?
2. Describe a time something broke in a build, a deploy or a server. How did you find the cause?
3. Tell me about a task you automated, such as a script or a pipeline step. Why did you automate it, and what did it save?
4. Tell me about a time you made a mistake in a system or a config. What happened, how did you fix it, and what do you do differently now?

### Mid-level (2 to 4 years)

1. Tell me about an incident you responded to on call. What did you check first, what was the cause, and what did you change afterwards?
2. Describe how you improved a CI/CD pipeline or a release process. What was slow or risky before, and how did you measure the change?
3. Tell me about a monitoring or alerting change you made. Which signals did you pick, why those, and did it cut noise or catch problems sooner?
4. Tell me about a trade-off between reliability and cost, or between reliability and speed of change. What did you choose, and why?

### Senior (5 years or more)

1. Tell me about the reliability of a system you were responsible for. What targets did you set, such as SLOs, and how did you decide them?
2. Describe a major incident you led or a postmortem you ran. What did you find, and which changes actually stuck?
3. Tell me about an infrastructure migration you planned, such as a new cloud, cluster or database. How did you lower the risk, and what went wrong anyway?
4. Tell me about a time you got other teams to change how they deploy or operate their services. How did you convince them, and what was the result?

## Mobile

### Junior (up to 2 years, a bootcamp, or a career change)

1. Tell me about a mobile app or screen you built, in a job, a bootcamp or your own project. What did you build yourself, and what was hard about it?
2. Describe a bug that only happened on some devices, OS versions or network conditions. How did you find it and fix it?
3. Tell me about a time you had to decide how an app stores or loads data, for example caching or working offline. What did you choose, and why?
4. Tell me about feedback from a user, a tester or a reviewer that changed something you built. What did you change?

### Mid-level (2 to 4 years)

1. Tell me about a mobile feature you owned from start to release. What made it hard, and what did you decide yourself?
2. Describe a time you fixed a crash, a slow screen, or high battery or data use. How did you measure it before and after?
3. Tell me about a release that went wrong or was rejected by an app store. What happened, and what did you change in how you ship?
4. Tell me about a trade-off between a native and a cross-platform approach, or between two libraries. What did you weigh, and how did it work out?

### Senior (5 years or more)

1. Tell me about the architecture of a mobile app you led. Which decisions mattered most, and what would you change now?
2. Describe a time you improved the stability or performance of an app with many users. What did the numbers look like before and after?
3. Tell me about how you handled releases, feature flags or older app versions still in use. What went wrong, and what did you learn?
4. Tell me about a time you worked with backend, design or product to change a feature for mobile users. How did you reach an agreement?
